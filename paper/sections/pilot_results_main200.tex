\section{Pilot Results (Main Study, Three Open VLMs)}

\paragraph{Method recap.}
We measure visual decision updates with the CertVIC protocol: for each reviewed item we
ask a presence question on the original image and on a single-factor edited image
(removal, occlusion, or displacement) that passed a detectability and quality gate, and
record the original-image accuracy $a$ and the post-edit answer consistency $p$. The
reported gap is $\Delta = a - p$, certified per run via an anytime-valid confidence
sequence on the bounded transform of the per-item gap at $\alpha = 0.05$.

\subsection{Per-edit-type discussion}
% Source: data/results/main_real_200/pilot_report*/presence_by_edit_type.json
% Removal shows the largest gap (post-edit consistency near zero); displacement and
% occlusion show smaller but consistent gaps. control_irrelevant (specificity) = not_available.
The gap is largest for removals, where the target is fully gone yet the presence judgment
often does not revise; displacement and occlusion show smaller but consistent gaps. The
\texttt{control\_irrelevant} (spurious-flip) specificity column is \emph{not available}: its
images and tasks exist but its VLM predictions have not been produced.

\subsection{Natural absence vs.\ edited absence (the dissociation)}
The control and intervention together describe a dissociation: the models reliably detect
\emph{natural} absence, yet frequently fail to revise a presence judgment after a
controlled, low-detectability \emph{edited} removal. This dissociation is the core pilot
observation and is consistent across the three models.
